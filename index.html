What Happened

In the spring of 1914, a travelling showman vanished from a Marseille boarding house, leaving behind an unpaid bill, a wooden camera and a notebook. For years before that he had toured Europe with exhibitions of moving pictures; wherever he played, audiences rioted, wept, or walked into rivers. The camera and the notebook drifted east with the war's debris and ended up in the back room of a Cairo antiquities dealer.

That is where Nathaniel Cross found them in 1923, scouting locations for an Egyptian picture. Cross is a self-made man of the movies — nickelodeons, then features, now vice-president of production at Imperial Pictures — and a man with a weak heart and a large appetite. The camera's brass plate read Exhibitions of the Last Sunset. The notebook, Le Masque du Dernier Couchant, was translated for him by a Pasadena occultist, Dr. Ignatius Pell, and it promised Cross what his doctors could not: that whoever stages the Masque, and lets enough eyes see it, will take a seat “in the dark theatre that has no last reel.”

The rite is a sexual one. It needs masked celebrants, a west-facing room at sunset, and a vessel — a young woman who says yes, however that yes is obtained, without knowing what she is really giving. So Cross built the Silver Circle: rich and powerful men — producers, a senator, a bandleader — and the aspiring actresses that Imperial's studio cars deliver to them, promised screen tests and parts. Robes and Latin make it a secret society; cameras behind a curtain give Cross a hold over every man there. To the young women it is one more thing they are told is normal. Two earlier vessels fainted, then recovered. Cross needed a stronger one.

Six weeks ago, Vivienne Ashcroft, a fading silent star who survived the same machine twenty years ago, brought her protégée to Cross's glamorous parties, telling herself she could protect her: Evelyn Vale, 22, from Dayton, Ohio, warm, ambitious and two small parts into a career. Within weeks Evelyn was drawn into the private nights, told it was the price of a career. Vivienne warned her, and did not stop her. Pell chose her as the vessel.

On Saturday, June 5, 1926, at sunset, Evelyn was led into the west salon wearing nothing but a gold mask. Cameraman Arthur Bell, hired privately and paid by Cross himself, cranked the showman's camera. As the sun touched the sea, Evelyn went limp among the masked men — and through the warm, dark lens Bell watched her still there, among the masks, while her body lay slack on the cushions. The camera had taken her mind onto the film. Bell fled.

The next day he went to the only honest man he knew outside the studio: Jack Mercer, the ex-cop turned private eye. Mercer smelled fear, took it for gin, and showed him the door. On Tuesday night, on Cross's order, his hired muscle Frank Dugan, an ex-deputy, ran Bell's roadster off Mulholland Drive. A Police Commissioner on Cross's payroll closed the case by morning.

Evelyn's body — breathing, unresponsive — was driven to Blackwater Lodge, Cross's retreat in the San Bernardino Mountains, where Nurse Whitcomb keeps it alive and Dugan keeps everyone else away. Cross does not fear her rescue. He fears her death: as long as Evelyn's body lives, every complete print of the film carries her, and whoever watches it to the end is pulled into her endless sunset orgy — and loses every restraint they had. If she dies, the film becomes only film.

At the studio lab, negative cutter Ruth Abernathy cut the negative to Pell's instructions over three nights and lost her mind doing it; she is in a rest home now, painting sunsets on the wall with her fingers. The finished reel has a harmless name — “Sunset Serenade” — and a harmless surface: cut by Pell from the Masque footage, it reads on paper as an exotic masquerade fantasy of veils, masks and bare shoulders, racy enough to sell tickets and tame enough to pass the state censors. Whoever watches it to the end sees far more than is on the frame. And it has a perfect hiding place: it will run before every showing of Imperial's million-dollar epic Queen of the Nile. Forty prints are being struck. On Sunday, June 20, at 10 pm, they leave Central Station by express for film exchanges across the country. On Friday, June 25, the lights go down in forty cinemas.

On Friday morning, June 18, Evelyn's brother Thomas climbs the stairs to Mercer's office with her photograph in his hand. A reporter, Billy Mulligan, is two steps behind him. Vivienne, who sent Thomas there, is on her way — carrying a secret.

Evelyn cannot be saved. The investigators can only decide what her ending will be — and how many strangers share it.

01 · At a Glance · One-shot, 4–5 hours

The Core

Cross is visibly the villain. His plan is the mystery. Find Evelyn Vale; learn why cameraman Arthur Bell was killed; uncover the cursed short hidden in a nationwide release — and face the fact that Evelyn cannot be saved.

Three Supernatural Facts

The Camera — Cross's eldritch camera captured Evelyn's mind on film at a masked rite. Her body lingers in a coma; she is in every frame.

The Film — Anyone who watches a complete print uninterrupted is drawn into the endless orgy of her sunset: erotic fixation, loss of all inhibition, violent obsession, psychic collapse. Every complete copy is lethal while Evelyn's body lives.

The Ending — Only two things break it: Evelyn dies, or every copy is destroyed. Nothing brings her back.

The Objective

Before Sunday 10 pm: destroy the master negative, all 40 prints and Cross's work print.

After the train leaves: only Evelyn's death stops opening night.
Cross's death alone stops nothing — Dr. Pell carries on.

GM Rules

  • Leads, not locks. A failed move adds trouble, cost or time — never a dead end.
  • The world moves. Cross reacts to leverage, not curiosity; the Red Clock runs on the calendar.
  • Fair play. No mandatory clue order or roll; if players guess early, let them act.
  • On a 6−, make a GM move: reveal an unwelcome truth, put someone on the spot, cost them resources, advance a clock.

Safety & Boundaries

Theme: the sexual exploitation of young women in Hollywood. Name it in Lines & Veils.

Never depicted: sexual acts or abuse — only what PCs find out and the aftermath. PCs are witnesses, never participants.

Off-screen: the screenings. Ask first how a mercy killing is handled.

02 · GM Only · The Truth

Cross, the Camera & the Rite

Where the Camera Came From

In 1923, scouting Cairo for an Egyptian picture, Nathaniel Cross bought a wooden camera from an antiquities dealer near the Khan el-Khalili. The dealer said it had belonged to a travelling showman who toured Europe before the Great War with “exhibitions” of moving pictures — exhibitions after which audiences rioted, wept or walked into rivers. The showman vanished in 1914.

The camera looks like a 1910s hand-crank model, but its lens is ground from a dark, faintly warm glass, and its brass plate reads, in French, Exhibitions of the Last Sunset. Overcome Fear serious, if studied closely.

How Cross Knows the Rite

With the camera came the showman's leather notebook, “Le Masque du Dernier Couchant”. Occultist Dr. Ignatius Pell translated it for Cross. It prescribes:

  • masked celebrants and a sexual rite in a west-facing room, filmed at the moment of sunset;
  • a vessel at its centre whose “yes” is required — however it is obtained — and who does not know what she is for;
  • afterwards, prints struck from the negative, “so the sunset may be shared.”

The Silver Circle provides the celebrants: powerful men, and the young actresses Imperial's cars deliver to them. Two earlier vessels, Lila Marsh and Betty Crane, fainted and recovered, remembering only “a long sunset”; Bentham paid them off. Evelyn did not recover. Vivienne saw both led in and said nothing; she thinks the occultism is window dressing.

Why Cross Does It

Public face: a liberal aesthete who says taboo imprisons humanity. In fact a predator who has used his studio for years to feed powerful men young women — the films are his hold over them.

Real motive: the notebook promises that when enough eyes have seen the sunset, the Showman returns — and the one who staged the Masque will take a seat “in the dark theatre that has no last reel.” Cross is 58, his heart is failing, and he believes this is immortality. Whether anything comes is the GM's secret; nothing has to.

The Plan

The short“Sunset Serenade”, an 11-minute “masquerade fantasy” (one reel) cut from the Masque footage: veils, masks, bare shoulders, passable for the censors. Seen complete, it shows the viewer the whole orgy, and draws them in.
The vehicleAttached as the programme short to every print of Imperial's summer epic “Queen of the Nile”. Exchanges and cinemas unpack both together.
The copies1 master negative (lab vault) · 40 release prints (lab, then depot) · 1 work print in Cross's salon.
The deadlineCans leave Sunday 10 pm on the Southern Pacific express. Opening night: next Friday, nationwide.
The anchorEvelyn's body at Blackwater Lodge, kept alive by Nurse Whitcomb. Cross needs her breathing for months.
His fearNot her rescue — her death. A hospital might lose her; a grieving brother might grant mercy. So: guards, secrecy, a nurse who asks no questions.
His successorIf Cross dies, Pell sees the shipment through.

Rules of the Curse

Anchor — While Evelyn's body lives, every complete copy is potent. Her death makes all prints mundane instantly.

Complete & uninterrupted — A print missing frames, a broken projection or a cut reel is harmless. Fragments and stills are unsettling, not lethal: Overcome Fear moderate. Nights of handling them, as Ruth did, wear a mind away piece by piece. Each time a still is viewed, Evelyn's face has turned a little further toward the camera.

Full viewing — Overcome Fear is not allowed: a permanent critical mental disorder (erotic fixation, compulsion to return to the sunset, catatonia). For a theatre audience: inhibitions collapse, then violence, fires, suicides — always off-screen.

Destroy every copy — When the last complete copy burns (nitrate burns fast and hot), the curse ends; Evelyn's mind burns with it. Her body lives on, empty.

No cure — There is no ritual of return. The notebook says so plainly: “The vessel is spent.”

05 · Scene One

Opening: Mercer's Office

Read aloud Friday morning, Los Angeles, 1926. The sun is already hot on Spring Street, and the Venetian blinds of the office on the fourth floor cut it into bars across a desk buried in unpaid bills and cold coffee. Downstairs, a newsboy hawks the latest Hollywood scandal. Upstairs, a big, sunburned young man in his one good suit stands in the doorway, turning his hat in his hands. In the other hand he holds a photograph: a pretty young woman with a film-star smile. "Mr. Mercer? My name's Thomas Vale. The studio says my sister's on vacation. Nobody back home has heard from her in two weeks. Evelyn doesn't do that."

Scene Beats

  1. The photograph. Thomas shows Evelyn's picture. Mercer recognises the name — the one Bell was raving about.
  2. Flashback. Ask Mercer's player to describe Bell in this office two weeks ago. Give them Bell's words: "She's still moving in there. Through the lens. He took her, Mercer — the camera took her."
  3. The press arrives. Billy knocks (he tailed Thomas from the studio gate) with what he's dug up: the studio's line that Evelyn is “on vacation” is rehearsed word for word, and Bell's “car accident” was closed by the Commissioner's office in a day.
  4. The confidante. Vivienne arrives — she sent Thomas here and wants to know what Mercer will do. Ask her player: what did Cross tell you about Palm Springs, and did you believe him?
  5. Pressure. The phone rings: an old police friend tells Mercer, as a friend, to stop asking about Bell. (A warning, not yet Amber 3.)

1926 in a Nutshell

  • Pictures are silent, with live orchestras. Every programme opens with a short — a comedy, a newsreel, a musical number — before the feature.
  • Film stock is cellulose nitrate: it burns faster than paper and can't be put out with water.
  • Prints go out from the studio lab by express train to regional film exchanges, then on to the cinemas.
  • Getting around: Red Car trolleys, taxis, a Ford if you're lucky. Telephones, telegrams and the evening papers carry the news.
  • Prohibition: everybody drinks; somebody always supplies it.

06 · Player Reference · Mythos World

Basic Moves

Roll 2d6 + attribute. 10+ success · 7–9 partial success · 6− the GM makes a move. Threat and injury levels: minor → moderate → serious → critical.

Search for Something +INT

Search an area or situation for an item or information. 10+ you find something useful. 7–9 you find it, choose 1: of limited use · you or an ally in danger · lose an opportunity · discover something frightening (moderate) · expend resources.

Consult Knowledge +INT

Draw on your knowledge. 10+ the GM tells you something interesting and useful. 7–9 interesting, but maybe not useful. The GM may ask, “how do you know this?”

Detect Truth +EMP

Observe or converse to learn the truth. 10+ ask 2. 7–9 ask 1, and suffer a setback or lose an NPC's trust: Who is in control here? What does someone really want? How does someone feel about my character? What happened here earlier? What is likely to happen next?

Convince +EMP

Control an NPC's actions or opinions when you have leverage. 10+ they do it if you promise to remove the leverage. 7–9 only after you prove the leverage is gone or do a task for them. Already willing? No roll.

Defy Danger MGT · SPD · WIL · EMP · INT

Avoid imminent danger or pass an obstacle: force +MGT, dodge +SPD, resist +WIL, charm +EMP, quick thinking +INT. 10+ you avoid it. 7–9 you hesitate; the GM offers 2, you pick: reduced effects · lose a resource · danger increases · face a horrifying situation.

Aid or Interfere +CON

Before another player rolls. CON = your connections with them. 10+ turn their 6− into 7–9 or 7–9 into 10+ (or the reverse). 7–9 you're exposed to danger; only 7–9 → 10+ (or 10+ → 7–9).

Attack up Close +MGT

10+ you injure the enemy; you may raise the injury one level and take one yourself. 7–9 you injure it, choose 1: it injures you · lose a valuable resource · danger increases.

Attack at a Distance +SPD

10+ you injure the enemy. 7–9 you injure it, choose 1: injury one level lower · lose ammunition or a resource · take damage from the enemy or its allies.

Overcome Fear +WIL

Faced with frightening creatures, situations or revelations. 10+ resist this and similar fears for the rest of the session. 7–9 resist for this encounter, choose 1: a mental disorder one level below the threat · a moderate injury · danger increases · you run away mindlessly · lose a resource. 6− the GM may give a disorder at the full threat level.

Cheat Death +MGT

On a critical injury or critical symptoms, if survival is possible. 10+ reduce to serious. 7–9 reduce to serious, choose 1: it becomes permanent · a permanent serious mental disorder · a nearby ally takes a serious injury. 6− you die.

Receive Money · Buy Something

Receive: check the money box equal to the Value (or any lower). Buy or give cash: uncheck the box equal to the Value (or any higher); if you can't, you can't buy it. Police Bribe Value 3 · Official Bribe Value 4.

Rest

A few days of physical inactivity: reduce one temporary injury a level. A few days without stress: reduce one temporary mental disorder a level.

End a Session

1 XP each: passion drove a major decision · a connection drove a major decision · experienced something beyond human knowledge · gained a moderate/serious disorder · gained a moderate/serious injury. Payday after a week. 10 XP → +1 attribute (max +2) or a new profession move.

Once clue 08 is known, someone will think it. Don't rush past that moment — it is the scenario's real climax.

  • Put someone on the spot: it should be a choice made at the bedside, ideally with Thomas present or deliberately kept away.

Cross, the Hitman & the Endings

Play Cross Intelligently

He assumes the PCs can prove nothing and reacts to leverage, not curiosity. Each of his moves exposes a weakness: a legal threat shows which evidence matters; surveillance can be spotted; moving Ruth leaves a trail. When he believes he has won, he tells them the truth — because the truth is the cruellest thing he owns.

Stage Six: The Moretti Contract

When the shipment itself is threatened, Cross asks Moretti to remove the “blackmailers”. Caruso hunts. Proof of Cross's films, brought via St. Michael's, cancels the hit. Nobody comes to help.
